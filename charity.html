Charity that can be checked.

Good intentions are not enough. BritBlue’s welfare mission is being designed around public records, clear updates and a simple question: can the community see what happened?

1

Identify

Support should go to credible cat-welfare needs, organisations or clearly documented cases.

2

Distribute

Charity funds are intended to be moved in a controlled, documented manner rather than through vague promises.

3

Report

Distributions and outcomes will be summarised publicly so holders and followers can review them.

Transparency commitments

  • Publish the charity wallet address and associated wallet register on the Treasury Transparency page.
  • Provide regular website updates covering welfare distributions and key project spending.
  • Link material on-chain transactions where practical so readers can independently inspect them.
  • Separate promotional language from confirmed numbers and completed distributions.

Public treasury information

BritBlue launched on 7 October 2026. The Treasury Transparency page provides the token contract address, public wallet register, and details of voluntary 12-month holding commitments. Wallet commitments are not blockchain-enforced locks. Any charity partnership or donation will be described as completed only when verified.

No private donation requests: BritBlue will not ask supporters to send funds to an address received through an unsolicited direct message.

The goal

The welfare mission is intended to grow with the project. Early support may be modest; the standard for reporting should not be. The project’s credibility depends on showing what was promised, what was actually distributed and what came next.
